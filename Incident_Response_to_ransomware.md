# Incident Handler's Journal — Ransomware at a Healthcare Clinic

## Journal Entry Details

- **Date:** October 10, 2026
- **Entry:** 1
- **Incident type:** Ransomware / phishing
- **Status:** Initial scenario review

## Description

This entry documents a ransomware incident at a small U.S. healthcare clinic. Employees were unable to access computers, medical records, and other files needed for normal operations. A ransom note stated that the files had been encrypted and demanded payment in exchange for a decryption key. The scenario indicates that the attackers gained initial access through targeted phishing emails containing a malicious attachment.

## Tool(s) Used

No cybersecurity tools were used in this scenario review. The information below is based on the incident description provided in the coursework.

## The 5 W's

### 1. Who caused the incident?

The incident was caused by an organized group of attackers described in the scenario as targeting healthcare and transportation organizations. The initial access was achieved by sending targeted phishing emails to clinic employees; the specific individuals responsible have not been identified.

### 2. What happened?

Ransomware was deployed after a malicious email attachment was downloaded, encrypting critical files and preventing employees from accessing medical records and other work-related systems. A ransom note demanded a large sum of money in exchange for the decryption key, and clinic operations were disrupted.

### 3. When did the incident occur?

The incident was discovered on a Tuesday at approximately **9:00 a.m.** The scenario does not provide a specific calendar date.

### 4. Where did the incident happen?

The incident affected a small primary-care healthcare clinic in the United States. The ransomware impacted the clinic's computer systems and files, including medical records required for day-to-day operations.

### 5. Why did the incident happen?

The attackers used targeted phishing emails to persuade employees to download a malicious attachment. Once the attachment was downloaded, malware enabled the attackers to deploy ransomware and encrypt important files, apparently to pressure the clinic into paying for a decryption key.

## Additional Notes

The clinic should preserve relevant evidence, isolate affected devices from the network where safe to do so, and coordinate its response with qualified incident responders and appropriate authorities. The organization should also determine whether backups are available and usable, identify the scope of affected systems and data, and assess whether patient information was accessed or exfiltrated; the scenario confirms encryption but does not establish data theft.

For follow-up, I would want to know which systems and accounts were first affected, whether the ransomware spread to backups, whether any data was exfiltrated, and what recovery procedures are available. These details would help determine the incident's full impact and guide recovery.
